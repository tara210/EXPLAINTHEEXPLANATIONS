Full lists and matching rules: attention/WORD_CLASSES.md in the repository. Ambiguous words are checked with spaCy: so is not counted in “so big”, since not before a number, like only as a comparison, then only after if/when.

How the comparison is made

Each word gets a score: the attention × gradient relevance it receives from all later tokens, averaged over its sub-word tokens and summed over layers. That score depends on much more than a word's class, so the model compares each class with similar words. Every row below removes one reason two words can differ; the evidence column is measured on the current data.

Limits and next steps

  • Attention, not meaning. Attention × gradient shows which attention helps the model predict the explanation; it does not show that the model “understands” a causal relation.
  • Eight classes are tested at once, so one interval excluding 1.0 could be chance. Effects that hold across both models and measures (causal, contrast, illustration, hedges) are the ones to trust.
  • Word lists are simple. The spaCy rules catch the commonest ambiguities; when (time vs condition) and actually (contrast vs emphasis) remain mixed.
  • No baseline of non-explanations yet. The effect may be typical of English prose in general rather than of explaining. Running the same measure on non-explanatory text would tell.
  • Next: highest- vs lowest-scored answers in the same model, which words drive each class, and per-head analysis.
